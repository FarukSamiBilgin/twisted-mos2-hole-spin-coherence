# Extended abstract (manuscript in preparation)

**Momentum-dark moiré excitons and an isotope-purification coherence budget for Γ-valley hole spins in twisted bilayer MoS2**
Faruk Sami Bilgin, Independent Researcher

The full manuscript is in preparation and is not included here. The published short version is in this folder.

I set out to use twisted bilayer MoS2 as a moiré qubit platform and found that it is dark by construction. Its valence-band maximum lies at Γ and its conduction-band minimum at K (or Q); for the lowest excitons to emit light, the moiré superlattice would have to supply about 16 of its reciprocal-lattice vectors (at θ = 2°) to bridge the Γ–K momentum gap, and the weak moiré coupling cannot do this. Four independent lines of evidence (published DFT and continuum theory, this momentum arithmetic, the photoluminescence record including photocurrent microscopy at 2°, and my own DFT cross-check) agree that the lowest-energy excitons cannot emit at any twist angle, although the material still absorbs at higher energy.

This rules twisted MoS2 out as an optical qubit, but the same band structure makes it a promising spin memory. The Γ-valley hole is a valley-free spin-1/2 without spin–orbit coupling, trapped by the moiré potential in a dot whose envelope covers only about 320 nuclei. In natural material this small number hurts (T2* ≈ 42 ns), but because the dot covers so few nuclei, purification with 98Mo and 32S leaves almost no spinful nucleus inside it. Using hyperfine constants transferred to Γ through my DFT orbital weights (56.7 % Mo d_z², 39.6 % S p_z), I find that purification lifts T2* into the microsecond class until the nuclei of the hBN encapsulation take over: their dipolar field sets a floor of 0.79–1.3 µs, so a highly enriched, hBN-encapsulated device reaches 0.7–1.0 µs, the level of natural silicon. Approaching isotopically purified 28Si (20–120 µs) would require moving the hBN nuclei further away with a spinless spacer.

Beyond the short version, the full manuscript contains closed-form expressions for the moiré well (barrier V1 − 8V2 + 9V3 = 77.4 meV, ħω = 57.0 meV) checked against diagonalization, a twist-angle scan from 2° to 4°, the GaAs method validation, and a six-row sensitivity analysis in which the radial-transfer factor dominates (0.75–3 µs around the 1.5 µs central value) and the K-band upper bound gives 0.14 µs.

Twisted MoS2 cannot talk to light, but for exactly that reason it is deaf to optical noise; nuclear spins and charge noise remain. I predict a 17–23-fold Ramsey contrast between natural and highly enriched devices (about 10-fold for standard 99.5 % 98Mo stock).
